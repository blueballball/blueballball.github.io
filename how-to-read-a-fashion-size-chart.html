How to Read a Fashion Size Chart Before You Buy

A practical method for comparing measurements and reducing avoidable returns when shopping online.

By the StyleChapter Editorial Team · 2026

Measure a garment you like

Your body measurements are useful, but a garment measurement can be more predictive for relaxed or structured clothing. Lay a well-fitting piece flat and measure key areas such as chest, waist, hip, rise, and length. Compare those numbers with the brand chart rather than guessing from a letter size.

Understand ease

The difference between your measurement and the garment measurement is ease. A shirt, tailored jacket, and knit require different amounts of ease to move comfortably. Product descriptions such as relaxed, slim, or oversized provide context, but always consider the fabric and construction too.

Check the model information carefully

Model height and the worn size can help you understand length and proportion, but they are not a substitute for measurements. Bodies and preferences vary. Use the information as one reference among several.

Look for construction clues

Stretch, adjustable waists, raglan sleeves, and wrap shapes can make fit more forgiving. Rigid fabrics, tailored shoulders, and fixed waistbands require more precision. If a brand provides garment measurements or fit notes, save them for future purchases.

Keep a personal record

Record the sizes and measurements that worked for you by brand and category. Over time, this becomes more reliable than generic size labels. It also makes online shopping quicker and more deliberate.
